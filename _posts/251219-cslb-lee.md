## **Summary of Complaint**

This complaint concerns **financial misconduct and dishonest dealing** by the contractor, including **misappropriation of funds**, **issuance of NSF checks**, and **failure to account for money received**.

------

## **Statement of Facts**

On **2025/06/05**, the contractor informed me that he was performing renovation work for the seller of a property located at **10106 Capistrano Ave, South Gate, CA 90280**, and represented the property as a good real estate investment. He invited me to inspect the property together with the seller’s realtor.

On **2025/06/10**, the contractor stated that the seller required a **$10,000 deposit** to secure purchase priority. He assured me the funds were safe, that I would not lose the money, and provided a **personal check as collateral**. Based on these representations, I issued a $10,000 check to the contractor. He stated that a city permit was pending and asked me to wait until early July before the sale could proceed.

On **2025/06/11**, I later discovered that the contractor **deposited my $10,000 check into his personal bank account**, instead of delivering the funds to the seller as represented.

On **2025/07/11**, the contractor continued to present investment opportunities and showed me another property under renovation at **8852 Syracuse Ave, Anaheim, CA 92804**, reinforcing his credibility and trustworthiness.

On **2025/08/09**, the contractor requested a **two-week loan of $15,000**, stating it was temporarily needed for renovation work on the Anaheim property and promising repayment by late August 2025. He stated that the property owner would pay him for the renovation work by that time. He again provided **personal checks as collateral**, and I issued three $5,000 checks to him.

Between **2025/08/14 and 2025/08/22**, the contractor deposited and cashed all three loan checks.

The agreed repayment deadline of **2025/08/31** passed with **no repayment**.

On **2025/09/04**, the contractor admitted that there was no money in his account and stated that the collateral checks would bounce, contradicting his prior assurances that the checks could be deposited at any time.

On **2025/09/23**, one of the collateral checks was returned **for insufficient funds (NSF)**. On **2025/09/24**, only **$5,000** was recovered, leaving **$20,000 unpaid**.

From **2025/09/10** onward, the contractor **stopped responding to all calls and messages** and remains unreachable.

------

## **Failure to Account for Funds**

At this time, I am unable to determine whether the funds I provided were **properly accounted for or delivered as represented**.

- I do not know whether the **South Gate property owner** was ever informed of or received the $10,000 deposit.
- I do not know **when the Anaheim property owner paid the contractor** for the renovation work, or whether payment was made prior to the missed repayment deadline.
- Despite repeated assurances of repayment, the contractor failed to return the funds and later issued checks that were returned for insufficient funds.

These facts demonstrate **misappropriation of funds**, **dishonest dealing**, **issuance of NSF checks**, and **failure to account for money received**, which warrant CSLB investigation and disciplinary action.

------

## **Current Status and Harm**

- **Outstanding unpaid amount:** $20,000
- The contractor remains unresponsive.
- The collateral checks provided by the contractor are unusable due to insufficient funds.

------

## **Request for CSLB Action**

I respectfully request that CSLB:

- Investigate the contractor’s conduct,
- Review the handling and accounting of funds received,
- And take appropriate disciplinary action as permitted under California contractor licensing laws.
