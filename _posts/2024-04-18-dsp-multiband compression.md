# Multiband Compression



**Multiband compression** is an advanced audio processing technique that divides an audio signal into several distinct frequency ranges or bands. [Each band can then be compressed individually, allowing for detailed control over the dynamic range of different parts of the audio spectrum](https://www.masteringbox.com/learn/multiband-compression)[1](https://www.masteringbox.com/learn/multiband-compression)[2](https://www.iconcollective.edu/multiband-compression-tips).

Here are the key points about multiband compression:

1. **How It Works**:
   - A **multiband compressor** essentially consists of a group of several compressors, each operating on a specific section of the full audio spectrum.
   - These sections are created using a **crossover**, which splits the audio into frequency bands below and above the crossover frequency.
   - For example, a two-band compressor might split the audio into everything below 200 Hz and everything above 200 Hz, each fed into a separate compressor.
   - You can extend this concept to create three-band or more complex multiband compressors with different settings for each frequency range.
2. **Why Use Multiband Compression**:
   - **Targeted Control**: Multiband compression allows you to address specific frequency ranges independently. For example, you can compress the bass separately from the midrange or treble.
   - **Problem Solving**: It’s useful for handling issues like excessive bass or harsh highs without affecting the entire mix.
   - **Mastering and Mixing**: Multiband compression is commonly used in both **mixing** (to shape individual tracks) and **mastering** (to enhance the overall mix).
3. **Setting Up Multiband Compression**:
   - When using multiband compression, consider the following:
     - **Crossover Points**: Choose appropriate crossover frequencies to divide the spectrum.
     - **Thresholds and Ratios**: Set different compression thresholds and ratios for each band.
     - **Attack and Release Times**: Adjust attack and release times based on the characteristics of each frequency range.
     - **Monitoring**: Listen carefully to the effect on the overall sound and make adjustments as needed.
